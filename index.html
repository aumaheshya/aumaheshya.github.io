      Research Vision · ARMOUR-MIND

      The support that helps most now may leave the weakest capability later.

      As extended reality (XR) and AI become a continuous cognitive layer across learning, work and everyday life, intelligent systems will decide what cue to provide, when to vary or fade it, when to restore it, and when to remain silent. My research asks when such support becomes an internal, durable route to knowledge, and how AI should govern it so that people keep their cognitive autonomy.

      • Routes · how external support becomes independent access to knowledge.
      • Resilience · telling dependence on help apart from robustness to disruption.
      • Autonomy · AI that knows when to step back.

      The brain around you shows the idea: the cyan XR scaffold fades as you go deeper, while semantic (teal) and spatial (violet) routes keep each memory (amber) alive, even when one route is disrupted.
