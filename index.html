        Academic Profile

        My research is guided by a simple ambition: that the support technology offers today can become a capability we carry tomorrow. At the intersection of people, computers, and human experience, I explore Cognitive, Affective and Agentic Incorporation as an XR Design Space: how what people perceive and remember (cognition), what they feel (emotion), and what they can choose and control (agency) can inform technology that adapts to them. I build and study extended reality (AR/VR/MR) systems that use AI and biosensing to respond to individual needs, with applications in skill learning, attention and distraction, and support for people with neurocognitive impairments. Central to my work is one question: how can adaptive XR enhance human agency rather than replace it, so that assistance in the moment strengthens capability over time and people learn, remember, and act with greater independence when technology steps back.
